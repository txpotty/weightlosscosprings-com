    How Naltrexone Gum Works for Patients in Colorado Springs, CO

    How Naltrexone Gum Works in Colorado Springs

    Naltrexone blocks opioid receptors, and the 50 mg tablet has been FDA-approved for alcohol and opioid use disorder for decades. A compounding pharmacy mixes a set amount of naltrexone into each piece of gum. While you chew, part of the dose may pass through the cheek lining and the remainder is swallowed, though nobody has measured that split well. That is the idea. Nobody has shown the gum works better than a tablet.

    Who Naltrexone Gum Is For

    Adults who would be reasonable candidates for naltrexone but dislike swallowing pills are the main group. Some ask about it as a low dose wellness option. Others want help with cravings as part of a broader plan, and like the idea of chewing a piece before a situation where cravings usually hit, such as a dinner out or the drive home from work. Opioid users are not candidates. Significant liver disease usually points to a different plan.

    Dosing and Use in Colorado Springs

    Your provider sets the strength of each piece and how often to use it. A typical instruction is to chew slowly for roughly 20 to 30 minutes and then discard the piece rather than swallowing it. One piece a day is common. Targeted plans use a piece shortly before a known trigger, with a written cap on how many pieces a day. Compounded strengths vary, so follow the label exactly.

    What to Expect in the First Weeks

    The first week is mostly about learning the routine. Mild nausea and headache are common early on with naltrexone in any format, and a handful of people sleep lightly for a few nights. Most of it settles by week two. The taste takes some getting used to. At a check-in around week two, your provider asks how many pieces you are using, whether the timing works and whether your gums feel sore.

    Side Effects of Naltrexone Gum in Colorado Springs

    The side effects match other forms of naltrexone and include nausea, headache, dizziness and trouble sleeping. The gum adds its own complaints, mainly a bitter taste and mild mouth or gum irritation. Opioids are the serious concern. Anyone who takes opioids, even now and then, can go into withdrawal on naltrexone, and opioid pain pills may stop working. Report yellowing skin, dark urine or severe stomach pain right away.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your questionnaire asks about current medications, opioid use, liver health, pregnancy and breastfeeding, plus any major medical conditions. Tramadol and codeine cough syrup are asked about by name. A recent liver panel showing ALT and AST may be requested when your history raises a flag. Jaw trouble matters too. Anyone with TMJ pain or fresh dental work may do better on capsules.

    How Naltrexone Gum Compares in Colorado Springs

    Standard naltrexone tablets have decades of research and FDA approval for alcohol and opioid use disorder. LDN capsules sit at the other end, compounded and off-label at a few milligrams a night. Gum is mainly a preference format, newer and much less studied, picked by people who find pills hard to take. Weight loss is a different story. For medical weight loss, GLP-1 drugs such as semaglutide have far stronger trial evidence than any naltrexone format.
